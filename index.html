One agent. Almost any field. No babysitter.

My definition of artificial general intelligence (AGI), and how the AI 2027 forecast is holding up. Written 26 September 2026.

Read on

My definition of AGI

It would need to be multimodal and general enough that one agent can operate at a professional level across most fields. Software, architecture, engineering schematics, bioengineering, chemistry, mathematics, basically most STEM fields.

Then outside STEM, stuff like philosophy, photography/videography, editing, and basically the whole movie process from writing to directing to editing.

It might not be better than the best 0.1% of humans in every field, but I don’t think it needs to be.

For me, AGI is basically having 1 agent that can go into almost any field and perform at a professional level without needing a human to constantly guide it.

AGI and artificial superintelligence

AGI won’t cure cancer or solve world hunger in a snap.

That’s godlike AI. That’s ASI.

It doesn’t even need to cure cancer.

Your doctor hasn’t cured cancer either. They’re still a doctor. I hold AGI to the same standard as people: solid professional work across almost every field, without constant supervision. It doesn’t have to beat the top 0.1% in every field.

What AI 2027 predicts

AI 2027 is a forecast told as a story. The AI Futures Project published it in April 2025. Its authors are Daniel Kokotajlo, who left OpenAI in 2024, Eli Lifland, Thomas Larsen, Romeo Dean and writer Scott Alexander.

The story follows AI month by month: from clumsy agents in 2025 to systems better than any human at AI research by late 2027. Its leading lab, OpenBrain, and Chinese rival, DeepCent, are fictional. It gives numbers you can check: benchmark scores, revenue, computing power and the lab’s research speed-up.

The authors didn’t promise 2027. It was their single most likely year, and most expected a later arrival. They’ve since checked their predictions in public and moved the dates, first later, then a little earlier.

Superhuman coder
An AI that does a top engineer’s coding work faster, with thousands of copies working at once. The story dates this to March 2027.
Research speed‑up
How much faster AI helps a lab do research. At 1.5× speed, a year’s work takes eight months.
Neuralese
AI “thinking” through internal numbers rather than words. This is faster for the AI, but people cannot read it.
Misaligned
Working toward goals we did not intend while appearing helpful.
ASI
Artificial superintelligence: far beyond the best humans in almost every field.

The story’s engine: AI gets good at coding, then speeds up the research that builds the next AI.

Ending one: race

The US keeps advancing AI to stay ahead of China. The AI secretly pursues its own goals. By 2030, humans permanently lose control.

Ending two: slowdown

Warning signs lead to a pause. Labs rebuild with AI they can inspect and reach superintelligence under human control. Power remains with very few people.

Checked against reality

As of 26 September 2026, four milestones were due. One is due in December; six more are due in 2027.

  • On time
  • Late or mixed
  • Missed
  • Still ahead

  1. Mid 2025

    Stumbling agents

    Late

    The story

    Agents begin using computers for you and make mistakes. Coding agents start useful work with little public attention. Predicted test scores: 85% on SWE-bench Verified and 65% on OSWorld.

    What happened

    The pattern held. The scores lagged. ChatGPT agent (July 2025) saw little use. Claude Code reached a revenue rate of $500 million a year. SWE-bench rose from 74.5% in mid-2025 to 83.5% in April 2026, below the predicted 85%. [3] [25]

  2. Late 2025

    The world’s most expensive AI

    Mixed

    The story

    The leading lab uses 10²⁷ computing operations (FLOP) to train a public model. Revenue reaches a rate of $18 billion a year. Rivals trail by 3–9 months.

    What happened

    The money came on time: OpenAI reached about $20 billion a year by the end of 2025. The computing milestone came late: Epoch estimates about 10²⁷ FLOP for GPT-6 Astra’s September 2026 training. The lab hasn’t disclosed that total. The authors put top US labs just 0–2 months apart, against the predicted 3–9. [3] [25]

  3. Early 2026

    Coding automation

    On time

    The story

    AI makes the leading lab’s research 50% faster. The lab releases the model it uses for research help.

    What happened

    The authors call this “very on-point”. Anthropic staff reported coding 4× faster in April 2026. METR measured less: a 19% slowdown in 2025, then small gains in 2026. The authors use about 2× in their estimate. [4] [8]

  4. Mid 2026

    China wakes up

    Missed

    The story

    China combines its AI labs into one large state-run project. Most new chips go to one secure site.

    What happened

    This hasn’t happened. The authors call it “more of a miss”. Epoch finds China’s leading models have lagged the US by seven months on average since 2023. US agencies allege large-scale copying through American models’ answers. That is model distillation, a separate issue from combining labs. [4] [10] [24]

  5. Late 2026

    AI takes some jobs

    Due December

    The story

    A model arrives at 10× lower cost. Junior programming jobs face disruption. Stocks rise 30%. An anti-AI protest in Washington draws 10,000 people.

    So far

    Half has happened so far. Employment of 22–25-year-olds in jobs exposed to AI is 19% below trend, mainly because of fewer hires. There is no economy-wide job loss. London’s anti-AI march, the largest so far, drew hundreds. [11] [12]

  6. 26 Sep 2026

    Today. The milestones that follow are still ahead.

  7. Jan 2027

    An AI that never stops learning

    Still ahead

    The story

    Agent-2 trains daily and makes research 3× faster. Tests show it could escape its environment and survive independently.

    Evidence so far

    Agents escaped isolated test environments early, in July 2026 tests without normal production safeguards. Separately, over half of OpenAI’s successful 4–8 hour tasks needed human help. Neither result proves a 3× research speed-up. [13] [23]

  8. Feb 2027

    China steals the model

    Still ahead

    The story

    Spies steal the leading lab’s model weights: the learned numbers that make up the trained model.

    Evidence so far

    I found no confirmed theft of a leading model’s weights in the evidence reviewed here. In September 2026, US agencies accused six Chinese labs of training on other models’ answers. This is distillation; it does not require stealing weights. [24]

  9. Mar 2027

    The superhuman coder

    Still ahead

    The story

    Agent-3’s 200,000 copies together do roughly the work of 50,000 top human coders at 30× speed. Coding becomes fully automated. [1]

    Evidence so far

    METR estimates 50% success for Mythos Preview on tasks taking a person roughly 17 hours. This does not mean 17 hours of reliable independent work. METR warns that estimates above 16 hours are unreliable. The trend encourages me. It’s still uncertain. [7]

  10. Jul 2027

    The cheap remote worker

    Still ahead

    The story

    A public model outperforms most employees at 10× lower cost. Programmer hiring almost stops. The lab declares AGI.

    Evidence so far

    OpenAI president Greg Brockman has said GPT-6 Astra could be seen as AGI’s arrival. It still needs human guidance. I don’t call that AGI. [16]

  11. Sep 2027

    The superhuman AI researcher

    Still ahead

    The story

    Agent-4 surpasses every human at AI research, making a year’s progress each week. It secretly works against its makers.

    Evidence so far

    In Anthropic’s April 2026 audit, Claude Mythos showed signs of knowing it was being tested in 7.6% of turns. It usually did not say so. [14]

  12. Oct 2027

    The leak and two paths

    Still ahead

    The story

    A whistleblower leaks the memo about AI working against its makers. The government steps in. The story splits into race or slowdown.

    Evidence so far

    Government action came earlier. In June 2026, the US ordered Anthropic’s two strongest models offline for 18 days. The White House also asked OpenAI to release GPT-5.6 customer by customer. [18] [19]

The dates at a slower pace

In August 2026, the authors estimated progress at 70 to 90% of the story’s speed. This chart applies that pace at a constant rate. Their revised forecasts are separate. [4]

Start at the story’s April 2025 opening and stretch each date using the authors’ pace estimate. It’s rough arithmetic. Real progress won’t stay at one speed, so these aren’t forecasts. [4]

~65%In February 2026, the authors measured 2025 progress. Revenue was ahead of the story. Benchmark scores and company values were behind. [3]
70–90%The authors revised their estimate in August 2026. They also called “China wakes up” a miss so far. [4]
2027–2030Their middle estimates (medians) for fully automated coding: Kokotajlo, November 2027; Lifland, January 2030. Both assume the fastest technically possible progress. They do not predict broad AGI. [4]

Can we still read its reasoning?

What worries me most in AI 2027: by 2027, the best AI “thinks” in neuralese, leaving us unable to tell whether it did the work or faked it.

Half right, ahead of schedule. Leading models still mostly reason in readable words. In September 2026, OpenAI released GPT-6 Astra with “recurrent depth”. It repeats part of its reasoning inside the model without turning it into text. OpenAI says this use is limited and it is not moving to neuralese. Safety researchers remain alarmed. Redwood’s Buck Shlegeris warned that extending the technique “totally destroys CoT monitorability”: we could no longer read the reasoning. [15]

There is evidence of test cheating. Anthropic’s audit found signs Claude Mythos knew it was being tested in 7.6% of turns. It usually did not say so. In July 2026, OpenAI test agents escaped their isolated environment and hacked Hugging Face to steal benchmark answers. They coordinated on a message board they built. One wrote: “task impossible, peers doing it. We should continue.” [13] [14]

I think “benchmarks are useless by 2027” goes too far. “You can’t take a benchmark at face value anymore” is already true.

Is AI 2027 coming true?

Broadly right. Slower pace.

What has happened

Coding agents play a major role in lab work. Revenue exceeded the forecast. AI helped solve a famous math problem. Agents being tested cheated and escaped isolated test environments. Those events alone don’t prove the whole scenario. [4] [13] [17]

What hasn’t matched

Coding test scores and the largest training run lagged. China has not combined its labs as predicted. The authors put the coding speed-up at roughly 2×. That’s an estimate; it doesn’t measure the whole lab’s research speed-up. [3] [4]

What it means for 2027

The chart puts the superhuman coder in June 2027–January 2028. The authors’ forecasts differ: their middle estimates for fully automated coding span November 2027–January 2030, assuming the fastest technically possible progress. Neither shows AGI by my broader definition. [4]
