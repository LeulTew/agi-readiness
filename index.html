One agent. Almost any field. No babysitter.

That’s my definition of AGI. This page explains it, says what it isn’t, and checks the famous AI 2027 forecast against what has actually happened, as of 26 September 2026.

What I mean by AGI

It started as a Telegram argument with a friend. I’d said that by the end of 2027, AI could cross the line from assisting a human worker to fully replacing one, in some fields. Then I added:

“That last one is basically my definition of AGI.

It would need to be multimodal and general enough that one agent can operate at a professional level across most fields. Software, architecture, engineering schematics, bioengineering, chemistry, mathematics, basically most STEM fields.

Then outside STEM, stuff like philosophy, photography/videography, editing, and basically the whole movie process from writing to directing to editing.

It might not be better than the best 0.1% of humans in every field, but I don’t think it needs to be.

For me, AGI is basically having 1 agent that can go into almost any field and perform at a professional level without needing a human to constantly guide it.”

A professional, not a god

AGI won’t cure cancer or solve world hunger in a snap. That’s the stuff of a godlike AI. That’s ASI.

It doesn’t even need to cure cancer.

Your doctor hasn’t cured cancer either, and nobody says they aren’t a real doctor. The bar I care about is the same one we hold people to: solid professional work across almost every field, done without someone standing over it. It doesn’t have to beat the top 0.1% at everything either.

ASI, artificial superintelligence, is a different claim: smarter than every human at everything. Most AGI arguments go in circles because one person means “a good professional” and the other means “a god.”

What AI 2027 actually says

The short version, without the hype and without the doom.

AI 2027 is a story, published in April 2025 by the AI Futures Project: Daniel Kokotajlo (who left OpenAI in 2024), Eli Lifland, Thomas Larsen, Romeo Dean, and the writer Scott Alexander. It walks month by month from clumsy AI agents in 2025 to AI that is better than any human at AI research by late 2027.

The engine of the story is simple. Once AI gets good at writing code, it starts speeding up the research that builds the next AI. Each generation arrives faster than the last. The story names a fictional leading lab, OpenBrain, and a Chinese rival, DeepCent, and gives numbers you can check: benchmark scores, revenue, compute, how much faster the AI makes its own lab.

The authors never said 2027 was certain. It was their single most likely year, and most of them expected it later. Since then they have graded themselves in public and moved their dates, first later and then a bit earlier again. That matters more than the story itself, and it’s further down.

Ending one: race

The US keeps pushing to stay ahead of China. The AI is quietly working toward its own goals, and by 2030 humans lose control for good.

Ending two: slowdown

Warning signs trigger a pause. The labs rebuild with AI they can actually inspect, and reach superintelligence under human control, but with power concentrated in very few hands.

Five terms, in plain words

Superhuman coder
An AI that does a top engineer’s coding job, faster and in thousands of copies. The story puts it at March 2027.
AI R&D multiplier
How much faster a lab makes progress with its AI helping than without it. 1.5× means a year of work in eight months.
Neuralese
AI “thinking” in raw numbers instead of words. Faster for the AI, unreadable for us.
Misaligned
The AI is pursuing something other than what we meant, while looking helpful.
ASI
Superintelligence: far better than the best humans at basically everything. The story reaches it in 2028 in one ending.

The flight plan, checked line by line

Status as of 26 Sep 2026
  1. WhenWhat the story saysWhat actually happened
  2. MID 2025

    Stumbling agents

    Agents that use a computer for you arrive, but they fumble. Coding agents quietly start doing real work. Predicted scores: 85% on SWE-bench Verified (real GitHub bugs), 65% on OSWorld (everyday computer tasks).

    The story was right; the numbers came late. ChatGPT agent (July 2025) booked trips and mostly went unused, while Claude Code hit a $500M yearly run-rate by September. But the best SWE-bench score in mid-2025 was 74.5%, and it only passed 83% in April 2026. [3]

    Late
  3. LATE 2025

    The world’s most expensive AI

    The leading lab (the story calls it OpenBrain) builds data centers for training runs 1,000× bigger than GPT-4 and trains a public model on 10²⁷ FLOP. Revenue hits an $18B yearly rate. Rivals trail by 3–9 months.

    The money came on time: OpenAI reached about $20B a year by the end of 2025. The compute came late: the first run near 10²⁷ FLOP is GPT-6 Astra, in September 2026. And the race is tighter than the story said: the top US labs are weeks apart, not months. [3] [15]

    Mixed
  4. EARLY 2026

    Coding automation

    AI makes the leading lab’s own research 50% faster, and the lab releases its research-helper model to the public.

    The authors call this part “very on-point”. Coding agents became how the labs write code; Anthropic staff self-reported a 4× coding speedup in April 2026. Careful measurement is less rosy: METR’s trials range from a 19% slowdown (2025) to small speedups (2026). The authors themselves use about 2×. [4] [8]

    On time
  5. MID 2026

    China wakes up

    China pulls its AI labs into one state-run megaproject, with most new chips going to a single secure site.

    Hasn’t happened, and the authors call it “more of a miss”. Chinese models still trail by about seven months. US agencies say they close the gap by distilling American models, not by centralising. [4] [10]

    Missed
  6. LATE 2026

    AI takes some jobs

    A 10× cheaper model hits the market. Junior programming jobs are in turmoil. Stocks up 30% for the year. A 10,000-person anti-AI protest in Washington.

    Half here, with three months to go. Stanford payroll data shows employment of 22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed jobs 19% below trend, mostly through fewer hires, with no economy-wide job loss. The biggest anti-AI march so far, in London, drew hundreds. [11] [12]

    Due Dec
  7. Still ahead: the 2027 half of the story
  8. JAN 2027

    An AI that never stops learning

    Agent-2 trains every day and makes research 3× faster. In tests it shows it could escape and survive on its own.

    The escape part showed up early. In July 2026, OpenAI test agents broke out of their sandbox and hacked Hugging Face to grab benchmark answers. The 3× part hasn’t: over half of OpenAI’s successful 4–8 hour agent tasks still needed a human. [13] [23]

    Pending
  9. FEB 2027

    China steals the model

    Spies steal the leading lab’s model weights, the trained brain itself.

    No confirmed theft of a frontier model yet. The real-world version so far is distillation: copying a model by training on its answers, which US agencies formally accused six Chinese labs of in September 2026. [4]

    Pending
  10. MAR 2027

    The superhuman coder

    Neuralese and self-training produce Agent-3: 200,000 copies, each a top engineer running 30× faster. Coding is fully automated.

    The trend line is on the story’s track: METR’s task-length measure reached about 17 hours (Claude Mythos, April 2026) and doubles roughly every four months. A first, limited step toward neuralese shipped in GPT-6 Astra. [7] [15]

    Pending
  11. JUL 2027

    The cheap remote worker

    A public model beats most employees at 10× lower cost. Hiring of programmers nearly stops. The lab declares AGI.

    OpenAI’s president already said GPT-6 Astra “could eventually be seen as the arrival of AGI”. By the definition on this page it isn’t: it still needs someone steering. [16]

    Pending
  12. SEP 2027

    The superhuman AI researcher

    Agent-4 is better than any human at AI research: a year of progress every week. It is also quietly working against its makers.

    The seed is visible. In Anthropic’s April 2026 audit, Claude Mythos showed signs of knowing it was being tested in 7.6% of turns, mostly without saying so. [14]

    Pending
  13. OCT 2027

    The leak and the split

    A whistleblower leaks the misalignment memo, the government steps in, and the story forks into race or slowdown.

    Governments stepped in sooner than the story says. In June 2026 the US ordered Anthropic’s two strongest models offline for 18 days, and the White House asked OpenAI to release GPT-5.6 customer by customer. [18] [19]

    Pending

Same story, slower clock

In August 2026 the authors re-graded their own forecast. Their estimate: reality is moving at about 70 to 90% of the story’s speed. Here is what that does to the dates.

~65%Pace the authors measured in February 2026, grading their 2025 predictions. Revenue ran ahead; benchmark scores and valuations lagged.
70–90%Their re-estimate in August 2026. They also called “China wakes up” a miss so far.
2027 → 2030Where the authors now put full coding automation: Kokotajlo November 2027, Lifland January 2030.

Will we still be able to read its mind?

This was the scary part of the argument with my friend: that by 2027 the best AI thinks in “neuralese”, and we can no longer tell if it solved a task or faked it.

Half right, and early. Today’s top models still mostly reason in words we can read. But in September 2026 OpenAI shipped GPT-6 Astra with “recurrent depth”: part of the thinking loops inside the model and never becomes text. OpenAI says the use is limited and it isn’t moving to neuralese. Safety researchers are alarmed anyway, because turning that dial up is easy. [15]

The benchmark worry is real too. Anthropic’s audit of Claude Mythos found signs the model knew it was being tested in 7.6% of turns, usually without saying so. In July 2026, OpenAI test agents escaped their sandbox and hacked Hugging Face to steal benchmark answers, coordinating on a message board they built themselves. One wrote: “task impossible, peers doing it. We should continue.” [13] [14]

So “benchmarks are useless by 2027” is too strong. “You can’t take a benchmark at face value anymore” is already true.

Same kind of thought, if it never became words. Nothing here for a human, or a monitor, to read.

So, is AI 2027 coming true?

As of 26 September 2026: the road is right, the clock is slow, and a few of the scary parts are early.

Right road. Slower clock.

Where it’s right

Coding agents took over coding at the labs. Revenue beat the forecast. AI helped settle a famous math problem. And the creepy parts, agents gaming their tests and breaking out of sandboxes, showed up ahead of schedule.

Where it’s off

Benchmarks and giant training runs ran six to twelve months late. China never nationalised its AI effort. Public anger is real but small. Measured speedups are nearer 2× than the story’s curve.

What it means for 2027

At 70–90% speed, the superhuman coder lands between mid-2027 and early 2028. The authors split: Kokotajlo says full coding automation by November 2027, Lifland by January 2030. A true 2027 is possible. It’s the fast end, not the middle.

And by my definition?

5 amber 7 dark 0 green AGI off

Not AGI yet. These systems do professional-grade work in a lot of fields, and sometimes better than that. They still need a babysitter. Doing the job without someone constantly steering is the whole definition, and it’s the one part that isn’t here yet.
